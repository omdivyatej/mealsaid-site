6 Cal AI alternatives that do not need a photo

Six calorie apps that log a meal with no camera, with prices read on 24 October 2026. MyNetDiary gives voice logging away free. Cal AI publishes no price.

Om Divyatej, founder of MealSaid

  • Apple lists one Cal AI purchase called Unlimited at five different prices, from $2.99 to $29.99, and Cal AI publishes no pricing page at all.

If you want Cal AI without pointing a camera at your dinner, the shortest answers are MyNetDiary, which gives voice logging away on its free tier, and MealSaid, which is my own app and takes a spoken sentence. Both cost nothing.

Cal AI itself is not camera-only. Its site says "Snap a photo, scan a barcode, or describe your meal", so the description route exists (Cal AI). Two of those three routes still need the camera, and all of them need a subscription.

I read every price and feature below on each company's own site or US App Store listing on 24 October 2026.

Does Cal AI work without the camera?

Yes, through the describe route, but the app is built around the photo and says so.

The App Store listing gives three steps to use Cal AI, and step two is "Snap a photo of your meal" (App Store).

The feature Cal AI sells hardest is the one that cannot work without a lens: "your phone's depth sensor calculates food volume" (Cal AI).

The same listing carries a line in capitals saying food scanning analysis requires a subscription, so the camera-free route is not a free route either.

What does Cal AI cost?

Nobody can tell you, including Cal AI. Apple lists one purchase named Unlimited at five different prices between $2.99 and $29.99.

Cal AI's website has no pricing page. What it has is a FAQ about cancelling, a FAQ about refunds, a FAQ about restoring a purchase and a button offering a three day free trial (Cal AI).

The In App Purchases panel on its US listing shows Unlimited at $2.99, $5.99, $9.99, $19.99 and $29.99, plus a $0.99 Streak Restore (App Store). Apple never says what period any of them buys.

The top price is ten times the bottom one for a product with one name. You find out which one you were shown after the trial starts.

Which apps log a meal without a camera?

All six alternatives do, and so does Cal AI. Four of them let you type into a search box for free, and only MealSaid turns a whole spoken sentence into a meal at no cost.

Which free tier actually does the most?

MyNetDiary, and it is not close.

Its own feature table marks the staff verified database, unlimited search, the barcode scanner, voice logging, exercise and water tracking, up to 108 nutrient fields and the charts as free, with no ads and no account required to start (MyNetDiary).

108 nutrientstracked on MyNetDiary's free tier, with no ads and no account neededMyNetDiary

The catch is what "voice logging" means there. Free voice logging finds foods; the version that turns a spoken meal description into structured diary entries sits under Premium Plus, at $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year (MyNetDiary).

Cronometer's free tier is the other strong one: all 95 nutrients, the whole database and unlimited barcode scanning, with ads (Cronometer). Its voice route costs $59.99 a year.

If free is the whole requirement, start with MyNetDiary and read what is actually free in each app before you install anything.

Is describing a meal more accurate than photographing it?

Nobody knows, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing.

Photos have been measured once properly. Researchers at the NIH weighed 102 meals to 0.1 g, photographed them and ran them through four apps; Cal AI came in 345 calories low per meal, the worst of the four, and Appediet came in 252 low (Healio). Our full write up of that study has the confidence intervals.

There is no equivalent test for spoken or typed descriptions. Not for MyFitnessPal, not for Cronometer, not for mine.

What I can say is where the error comes from in both cases: portion size. A photograph does not show the oil absorbed into the food, and neither does the sentence "chicken and rice".

Which one would I use?

MyNetDiary if I wanted a food database and a barcode scanner for nothing. MealSaid if I just wanted the meal logged and gone.

Cronometer Gold sits between them: $59.99 a year buys the spoken meal plus the deepest nutrient data of the six, which is covered in our MacroFactor and Cronometer comparison.

Mine is the narrow one. You say what you ate and MealSaid writes calories, protein, carbs, fat and fiber into Apple Health. At times you pick, your phone rings and a voice asks what you ate.

It has no camera features at all, which is the point of this post, but that also means no barcode scanner, no nutrition label scanner, no food database to search and no photo logging. It is iPhone only and needs iOS 26. English only, too. On raw features MyNetDiary beats it, free, and I would say so to anyone who asked.
