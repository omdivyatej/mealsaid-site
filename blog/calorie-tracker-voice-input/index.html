Calorie tracker with voice input: what you actually get

"Voice input" in a calorie app means one of three different things, and only the third saves you any real time: dictating into a search box, speaking one food into a search, or saying a whole meal that comes back as several diary entries. Of the six iPhone trackers I found with that third kind, five charge for it, a median of $79.99 a year.

I read each app's own help pages and US App Store listing on 21 October 2026 rather than trusting the marketing word "voice". MealSaid is my app, so treat the other six rows as careful docs reading and not a hands-on test.

What does voice input mean in a calorie app?

Three levels, and apps use the same word for all of them. Level one is the iOS Dictate key, level two is a microphone that searches for one food, level three is a spoken sentence that becomes a set of entries.

Level one costs nothing and belongs to Apple, not the app. Any app with a text field gets it, because the keyboard has a Dictate key (Apple). You still search, and you still pick the match and the serving.

Level two is a microphone button wired to the same search. Lose It calls it Say It, and its own logging guide puts it plainly: "use Say It to search for a food by voice" (Lose It).

Level three is the one people picture when they imagine talking to a tracker. You say the sentence you would say to a friend, and the app hands back several foods at once.

Which calorie apps let you speak a whole meal?

Six that I could confirm from their own documentation: MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, MyNetDiary, MacroFactor, Lifesum and MealSaid. Lose It has voice, but only the search kind.

MyFitnessPal is the clearest writer of the group. Its help page says "Voice Logging searches the same food database you'd search by typing", then lists what is not supported yet: saved foods, recipes, meals, Quick Add, weight, exercise and water (MyFitnessPal).

MacroFactor's guide is equally blunt about the mechanism: "Just type (or speak) a description of your food or meal" (MacroFactor). The speaking part is just the keyboard; the matching is the feature.

What does spoken meal logging cost a year?

Between $59.99 and $99.99 a year in every app except mine, with a median of $79.99. Cronometer Gold is the cheapest paid route at $59.99 a year (Cronometer).

MyNetDiary is the interesting case, because it sells both levels. Its free tier page lists voice logging among the free features, while its App Store listing puts "AI voice logging: describe your meal out loud" under Premium Plus (App Store).

Premium Plus is $99.99 a year against $59.99 for plain Premium (MyNetDiary). A company with no reason to draw the distinction has drawn it, in its own price list, which is why I trust it more than any marketing page here.

MacroFactor has no free tier at all, so its $71.99 a year buys the app as well as the Describe feature (App Store). Lifesum's cheapest annual SKU is $99.99, with a $119.99 one also listed (App Store).

Can I log by voice without opening the app?

Only Cronometer and MealSaid document it, and both do it through Siri. Cronometer's support article says the feature "allows you to log foods using Siri without having to open the app", for Gold users on iOS (Cronometer).

Everyone else puts the microphone behind a screen. MyFitnessPal's numbered steps begin with opening the app and tapping the plus icon, Lifesum's AI flow opens from the plus button in the Diary, and MacroFactor's Describe lives inside the food logger.

That gap matters, because the tap people skip is the first one. I walked the routes on my own phone in the post on logging without opening an app.

Cronometer does one thing here that I like: it tells you to review Siri-logged foods the next time you open the app, and marks them with a Siri icon. Honest about the failure mode.

Is plain dictation good enough on its own?

For one food, yes, and it is free in every app. For a plate with five things on it, you are doing five searches with your voice instead of your thumbs.

Apple's guide warns that "Dictation may not be available in all languages or in all regions" (Apple). On supported models it runs without an internet connection, which no server-side voice feature can match.

One privacy footnote. Apple says dictated audio is sent to and processed on its servers unless your device shows in Keyboard Settings that it runs on device (Apple). So "voice" is rarely local, whichever app you use.

Which one should I pick?

If you already pay for a tracker, use the voice feature you have bought; if you are choosing from scratch, pick on what happens after you speak. The question that sorts the field: does it take a whole sentence, or one food at a time?

Then, can you start it without opening the app? Whether the app shows you its matched foods before saving them only starts to matter once you log every day, and by then it matters a lot.

Cronometer passes all three, for $59.99 a year, and it has the deeper nutrient data too. If I were paying, that is where I would start, and our MyFitnessPal alternatives table has the rest of its free tier.

MealSaid is my answer to the same three tests, for free. You say what you ate, it estimates calories, protein, carbs, fat and fiber, and writes them to Apple Health; a Siri phrase does it with the app closed. It has no barcode scanner and no photo logging, there is no food database to browse, it is English only, and it needs iOS 26. Wrong app for anyone who logs packaged food by barcode.

How I checked this

Checked 21 October 2026. I read each company's own help article or US App Store listing on that date and took the prices from the listing or the pricing page, not from a roundup. MyFitnessPal's help article was last updated 21 September 2026 and Cronometer's voice-logging article on 20 October 2026, per their help centres.

Two apps are missing for a reason. FatSecret's help pages and premium page returned 403 to every fetch I tried, so its Smart Assistant is not in the table rather than guessed. Questions

Which calorie trackers have voice input on iPhone?

MyFitnessPal, Lose It, Cronometer, MyNetDiary, MacroFactor, Lifesum and MealSaid all document some form of it as of 21 October 2026. What they mean by it differs, from dictating into a search box to saying a whole meal.

Is voice logging free in MyFitnessPal?

No. MyFitnessPal's own help page says Voice Logging is for Premium users on iOS and Android with English set, which is $19.99 a month or $79.99 a year on the US App Store.

Can I log food by voice without opening the app?

Cronometer Gold and MealSaid are the two whose own docs describe it, both through a Siri phrase. Every other app I checked puts the microphone inside a screen you have to open first.

What is the difference between voice logging and AI voice logging?

MyNetDiary draws the line clearly. Plain voice logging is dictation into a search, and AI voice logging turns a spoken meal description into diary entries. Only the second one is free of searching and picking.

Is MealSaid's voice logging free?

Yes, the whole app is free with no in-app purchases. It also has no barcode scanner and no photo logging, there is no food database to browse, it is English only, and it needs iOS 26.
