Yes, on 11 of the 21 iPhone food trackers I checked, plus my own. Eight of those eleven describe taking a whole spoken meal rather than one food at a time, and three apps take the meal through Siri while the app stays shut.

I read every one of those App Store listings and help pages on 27 October 2026. The listings came from Apple's own lookup service, which also gives the date each one was last changed, and that date turned out to matter.

One app contradicts itself. Lose It's listing was updated on 26 October 2026, the day before I read it, to claim whole meal voice logging, while its own help article from August still describes voice as a way to search for one food.

## Which iPhone apps can you actually talk to?

**Eleven of 21, and MealSaid makes twelve.** Nine of those twelve, mine included, say the app takes a whole spoken meal; ten apps say nothing about voice at all.

## What happens after you stop talking?

**Two things, depending on the app: a list of matched foods you approve, or a search result you still pick from.** Nothing in this field saves your words without showing you a guess first.

MyFitnessPal is the plainest about it. Its help article says "Voice Logging lets you say what you ate and get it into your diary", then walks you through tapping each food to fix the serving before tapping Log ([MyFitnessPal, 21 September 2026](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/30332897072269-How-to-log-food-with-your-voice)).

Cronometer describes the same shape, as a feature that lets you "dictate or type out a description of the food you are eating" and returns matched foods to review ([Cronometer, 26 October 2026](https://support.cronometer.com/hc/en-us/articles/47549391461780-Mobile-Voice-Logging)).

Two apps claim voice and leave it there. Carb Manager's Premium page lists "Say (voice logging)" with no explanation, and BiteSnap's listing calls itself a voice enabled food tracker and stops.

MacroFactor is honest that the microphone is incidental. "Just type (or speak) a description of your food or meal", says its logging guide, which is the keyboard doing the speaking and the search doing the work ([MacroFactor](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/215-how-to-log-food-in-macrofactor)).

## Which apps take the meal without opening the app?

**Cronometer on Gold, MacrosFirst on Premium, and MealSaid.** All three go through a Siri phrase, so the screen never has to come up.

Cronometer's Siri article, updated 24 October 2026, walks you through turning on its shortcut and then saying "Log food in Cronometer", after which Siri asks what you ate ([Cronometer](https://support.cronometer.com/hc/en-us/articles/47664884315412-Mobile-Siri-Voice-Logging)).

MacrosFirst does the same for Premium users, "all through Siri without ever opening the app", and handles serving size inside the Siri conversation ([MacrosFirst](https://help.macrosfirst.com/en/articles/75-siri-shortcuts)).

Everyone else puts the microphone behind a screen. I counted the actual touches for each route in [the hands free post](/blog/hands-free-calorie-tracking/).

## Why do two of Lose It's own pages disagree?

**Because the App Store listing was rewritten on 26 October 2026 and the help article has not caught up since 25 August.** That is 62 days between two pages from the same company saying different things.

The listing now puts "Say 'I had 2 eggs, toast with butter and jam' to log your meal" under Premium plan features ([Lose It, US App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lose-it-calorie-counter/id297368629)).

The help article still tells you to "use Say It to search for a food by voice", then pick the food, the serving unit and the quantity ([Lose It help, 25 August 2026](https://loseit.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/49210381551380-How-to-Log-Food-in-Lose-It)).

![Dated dot chart of when each company last updated the page its voice claim appears on, from Carb Manager on 10 July 2026 to Cronometer, MyNetDiary and Lose It on 26 October 2026, with the two Lose It pages 62 days apart](/blog/img/can-i-log-food-by-talking-to-an-app-2.webp)

I am taking the listing as the newer fact and the help article as the one to watch. If you are paying for Lose It Premium to say whole meals, try it before the renewal lands.

!note Apple's lookup service returns the listing text and a last updated date for any App Store id, which is how the dates above were read without a browser. It is the same data Apple shows on the product page.

## Which apps do not let you talk at all?

**Cal AI, Yazio, Noom, FatSecret, Nutritionix Track, Simple, Fastic, Zero, AteMate and MyPlate.** None of them mentions voice or Siri on any page I could open.

Cal AI is the notable one, because it sells itself on effortless logging. Its listing of 20 October 2026 offers a photo of your meal and a typed description, and never a spoken one ([Cal AI, US App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/id6480417616)).

FatSecret belongs in this column with an asterisk. Its listing of 6 October 2026 says nothing about voice, and its help centre returns 403 to every automated fetch I tried, so I am not going to describe a page I could not read.

BiteSnap sits at the other edge. Its listing calls it a "voice-enabled food tracker" and stops there, and that listing has not changed since 10 October 2025 ([BiteSnap, US App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bitesnap-ai-calorie-counter/id6747975537)).

## Which one should I talk to?

**If you already pay for a tracker, use the voice feature you bought; if you are starting fresh, decide first whether you want Siri or an in app microphone.** That single choice removes most of the list.

For Siri with the app shut, the paid answer is Cronometer Gold, and it has the deepest nutrient data of anything here. Cronometer is the better tool if you want numbers you can audit, and I say that as a competitor.

MealSaid is my free answer to the same question. You say what you ate, it estimates calories, protein, carbs, fat and fiber and writes them to Apple Health, and a Siri phrase does it with the app closed.

It has no barcode scanner, no photo logging, no food database to browse and no restaurant menus. It is iPhone only and needs iOS 26, and it understands English only. Wrong app for a household that logs packaged food by barcode.

## How I checked this

Checked 27 October 2026. I built the list by running US App Store searches for the names people use for calorie apps, keeping every general food or calorie tracker that came back, then reading each listing through Apple's lookup service and each company's own help centre.

Help centre dates come from the Zendesk timestamps on the MyFitnessPal, Lose It and Cronometer articles, read through those help centres' own article endpoints because the web pages return 403 to scripts, and from the published date on the Carb Manager page. MacroFactor and MacrosFirst show no date on their help pages, so those two rows say so rather than guessing.

Two gaps. FatSecret's help pages refused every request, so FatSecret sits in the no voice column rather than being described from a page I could not open. I did not install these apps or time them, so every row here is documentation reading, not a hands on test, and my own app is the only one I can speak about from use.
